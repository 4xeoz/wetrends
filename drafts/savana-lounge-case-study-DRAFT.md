## 3. The challenge

Most restaurants open with something to build on — a location that does the marketing for them, or a name people already know. Savana Lounge had neither.

The site was the problem the business was founded around: a room with no passing trade, on a street people don't walk down to eat. `[FAKE: "street people don't walk down"]` For a restaurant, that removes the single largest source of new customers. A high-street unit converts strangers into diners for free, every day it's open. Savana had to buy that reach some other way, or build it.

And it was starting from absolute zero. No logo, no photography, no website, no Google listing, no social presence, no reviews. To someone searching "restaurants in Guildford," Savana did not exist — not ranked low, not there at all.

That's the real brief: when footfall can't find you, every customer has to arrive deliberately. Which means the brand has to do the work the location won't.

## 4. Approach

**01 — Position around the location, not against it**
A venue people won't stumble into needs a reason to be sought out, so we built the positioning around being a destination rather than a convenience. `[FAKE: the specific strategic idea — what is the actual reason to seek Savana out? Cocktails? A room? A menu? An occasion? This is the sentence the whole case study hangs on and I've guessed it.]`

**02 — An identity from nothing**
Name treatment, palette, typography, menu design, signage and photography direction — the complete visual system, built from a blank page. For a business with no reputation, the identity is the only credibility signal a first-time customer has to go on.

**03 — Make the internet the front door**
Website, booking flow, Google Business Profile, and the social presence — set up so that a search for the venue, or for somewhere to eat in Guildford, lands on something that looks established. This is the substitute for footfall: the discovery a better location would have provided for free.

**04 — Launch**
Everything live at once, so the venue opened looking like a business that had been there for years rather than one still finding itself.
